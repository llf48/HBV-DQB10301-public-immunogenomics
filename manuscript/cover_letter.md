# Cover Letter Draft

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Public HBV genotype B/C core
diversity preserves an HLA-DQB1*03:01-associated class-II antigen-presentation
gap" for consideration.

HLA-DQB1*03:01 has recently been linked to increased risk of HBV-related
hepatocellular carcinoma, and prior work suggested reduced predicted binding of
HBV nucleocapsid peptides by DQB1*03:01-containing HLA-DQ molecules. However,
it has remained unclear whether this finding reflects a reference-sequence
artifact or a presentation gap that persists across naturally occurring HBV
genotype B/C diversity.

In this public-data computational study, we analyzed 469 quality-controlled
GenBank HBV genotype B/C complete-genome records, generated 5670 unique core
15-mer peptide windows, and performed HLA-DQ heterodimer MHC-II binding
prediction using the IEDB NetMHCIIpan workflow. We found that a DQB1*03:02
comparator heterodimer had substantially higher predicted HBV core binding than
two DQB1*03:01-containing heterodimers in both unique-peptide and occurrence-
weighted analyses. This gap was preserved in genotype B and genotype C
stratified analyses.

The manuscript is positioned as a hypothesis-generating public-data
immunogenomics study. We do not claim experimental proof of antigen processing,
surface presentation, or CD4 T-cell recognition. Instead, the study provides a
reproducible computational extension of the DQB1*03:01-HBV-HCC nucleocapsid
axis into public viral sequence diversity and nominates the HBV core region as
a focused target for future immunologic validation.

All data used in this study are public, and the analysis scripts and result
tables are prepared for deposition with the manuscript.

Thank you for considering our work.
