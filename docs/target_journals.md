# Target Journals

## Best-Fit Targets

1. **Viruses**
   - Fit: HBV, viral immunology, immunoinformatics, public sequence analysis.
   - Pros: open to computational virology and epitope prediction.
   - Risk: reviewers may ask for stronger biological validation or broader HBV
     protein coverage.

2. **Frontiers in Immunology**
   - Fit: antigen presentation, HLA, host-pathogen immunogenomics.
   - Pros: accepts hypothesis-generating computational immune studies.
   - Risk: may expect more HLA alleles, processing prediction, or immune
     validation.

3. **Journal of Medical Virology**
   - Fit: HBV sequence diversity and clinically linked viral immunology.
   - Pros: suitable for public-data virology studies.
   - Risk: HLA-DQ binding prediction may be considered too immunology-centered
     unless the HBV clinical rationale is very clear.

4. **BMC Genomics**
   - Fit: reproducible public-data genomics and host-pathogen analysis.
   - Pros: transparent pipeline and public data are strengths.
   - Risk: may want broader genome-wide HBV analysis, not only core.

5. **Hepatology Research**
   - Fit: HBV-HCC and Asian HBV genotype context.
   - Pros: connected to recent HLA-HBV-HCC literature.
   - Risk: a purely computational paper may need cautious framing.

## More Conservative Targets

- **BMC Infectious Diseases**
- **PLOS ONE**
- **PeerJ**

These are more realistic if the final manuscript remains prediction-only and
does not add independent patient cohorts or experimental validation.

## Submission Strategy

First-choice route:

1. Submit to **Viruses** if the manuscript keeps a virology/immunoinformatics
   framing and includes all code/results tables.
2. If rejected for lack of experimental validation, revise for **Journal of
   Medical Virology** or **BMC Genomics**.
3. If reviewers ask for too many new biological experiments, move to **PLOS ONE**
   with a transparent public-data framing.
