# Cover Letter

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled "Public HBV genotype B/C core
diversity reveals an HLA-DQB1*03:01-associated class-II presentation gap" for
consideration.

HLA-DQB1*03:01 has recently been associated with increased risk of
HBV-related hepatocellular carcinoma, and prior work suggested reduced predicted
binding of HBV nucleocapsid peptides by DQB1*03:01-containing HLA-DQ molecules.
However, it has remained unclear whether this finding reflects a reference-
sequence observation or a presentation gap that persists across naturally
occurring HBV genotype B/C sequence diversity.

In this public-data computational study, we analyzed 1576 quality-controlled
GenBank HBV genotype B/C complete-genome records, generated 11176 unique HBV
core 15-mer peptide windows, and performed HLA-DQ heterodimer MHC-II binding
prediction using the IEDB NetMHCIIpan workflow. We found that two
DQB1*03:01-containing heterodimers were marked low-binding outliers in an
expanded seven-heterodimer HLA-DQ panel, with occurrence-weighted core binder
rates of 0.23%-0.80% compared with 7.66%-13.52% for non-risk comparison
heterodimers. This gap persisted after exact and identity-based sequence
de-redundancy, record-level bootstrap analysis, genotype/region/year
stratification, and IEDB recommended-method sensitivity testing.

We also integrated IEDB human HBV T-cell epitope records and a reference HBV
proteome control analysis. Predicted NetMHCIIpan binders from the
DQB1*03:01-containing risk pairs did not overlap known human core/nucleocapsid
MHC-II epitopes, while the DQB1*03:02 comparator and other non-risk comparison
heterodimers did. Core-position analysis further localized the strongest gap to
HBV core N-terminal windows, generating a focused candidate map for future
experimental testing.

The manuscript is positioned as a hypothesis-generating public-data
immunogenomics study. We do not claim experimental proof of antigen processing,
surface presentation, CD4 T-cell recognition, or HBV-HCC causality. Instead,
the study provides a reproducible computational extension of the
DQB1*03:01-HBV-HCC nucleocapsid axis into public viral sequence diversity and
nominates HBV core antigen presentation as a focused target for future
validation.

All data used in this study are public, and the analysis scripts, processed
tables, and figure files are prepared for repository deposition.

Thank you for considering our work.
